The Omni-Swarm Protocol

Verified Knowledge Negotiation for Very Small Language Models at the Edge

Technical paper · OSP v0.6 "Connect" · Tree4Five — Omni-Swarm Protocol project · September 2026 · informative overview of the specification set (v0.4 · v0.5 Lite · v0.6 requirements · v0.6 architecture)

Abstract

Very small language models (0.5–1.5 B parameters, 4-bit quantised) running on phones, tablets and single-board computers hallucinate more, cost more per token, and hold less knowledge than their data-centre relatives — yet they sit exactly where personal documents already live. The Omni-Swarm Protocol (OSP) is a stateless, peer-to-peer knowledge-negotiation protocol that lets such devices answer each other's questions only from evidence they verifiably hold, and lets the asker independently verify every answer it accepts. This paper presents the protocol's design principles (verification-not-trust, generation-once, explicit failure, cheapest-decision-first), its mechanics (signed zero-state envelopes, centroid routing, contracting auctions with gas, a bounded negotiation lifecycle), its three-layer hallucination firewall anchored in recent calibration research [R1][R2][R6][R15][R16], and its economics (a decision ladder that cuts SLM invocations from ~6 per query to exactly 1). We report three interoperable implementations in Python, Kotlin and JavaScript (87 conformance tests, zero runtime dependencies, cross-language golden vectors) and a live end-to-end validation across an Android tablet origin, a JavaScript responder with vector retrieval and a local 12 B generator — including the negative result that matters: a text-less video answer is rejected by the origin-side groundedness gate with score 0.00. The protocol's stated posture is deliberately honest: every limitation, from miscalibrated bid signals to conformal distribution shift, is part of the specification.

Keywords: peer-to-peer protocols · small language models · edge AI · hallucination detection · confidence calibration · retrieval-augmented generation · ontology matching · semantic routing · conformal prediction

1 · Introduction

The current deployment geometry of language models is inverted with respect to where knowledge lives. The largest models run in data centres, reachable only through a network round trip; the documents that matter most to a person — payslips, manuals, correspondence, photos' captions — sit on that person's own devices, unreachable from the data centre by design and often by law. Meanwhile the devices themselves have become capable of running 4-bit quantised models of 0.5–1.5 B parameters, at 5–12 seconds per 200-token generation. What these tiny models lack is not storage (a phone holds gigabytes of personal text) but two things: reliable access to the right fragment at question time, and trustworthy speech — the ability to say an answer and be believed.

OSP addresses both by treating knowledge exchange as a negotiation between mutually untrusted peers, in the tradition of contract-net and FIPA-style agent interaction, but with the verification machinery of the modern hallucination literature bolted into the protocol itself rather than delegated to model introspection. The premise is that a 0.5 B model cannot be trusted to know what it does not know: calibration is poor at this scale [R6][R15], and the incentive structure of generative training favours guessing over abstention [R16]. Therefore the protocol, not the model, must carry the epistemic weight.

The result is a protocol in which:

  • a device that cannot generate at all (a thin phone, a bot client) can still ask and independently verify the answer it receives;
  • a device that can generate may only sell answers grounded in evidence it actually holds and cites;
  • every negotiation terminates in a bounded number of rounds and in one of six explicit convergence modes — silence and ungrounded guesses are both protocol violations, not UX accidents;
  • implementations in different languages interoperate byte-for-byte over one canonical wire format.

This paper synthesises the three-part specification set — the full profile v0.4 ([1]), the edge profile v0.5 Lite ([2]), and the v0.6 "Connect" requirements and architecture ([3], [4]) — into a single narrative, and reports the implementation and validation status as of September 2026.

2 · Design Principles

Principle 1

Verification, not trust

No node is trusted. The origin trusts only what it can check itself: packet signatures, its own retrieval-competence gate, its own groundedness computation over cited evidence, and reputation it accumulated per sender. Generators — local or remote — are untrusted downstream components (REQ-F-02): their output is data that must survive verification, never authority.

Principle 2

Generation once

On a mid-range phone a 0.5–1.5 B generation costs 5–12 s and real battery. OSP's cost structure is therefore inverted relative to chat-style RAG: only the auction winner generates, exactly once. Every other decision in the lifecycle — routing, gating, scoring, quorum, verification — is pushed down a ladder of progressively cheaper mechanisms (§7).

Principle 3

Failure is explicit

Abstention, gas exhaustion, loop detection, TTL expiry, evidence drift and ungrounded answers all terminate the negotiation as an RFO (Request-Failure) packet backtracked to the origin, or as one of six named convergence modes. A protocol that rejects answers must be able to show why (REQ-NF-04): every query carries a retrievable audit trace.

Principle 4

Zero-state envelopes

Nodes store zero session memory. The full negotiation state travels inside a signed envelope with a unique nonce (jti) and a TTL, so any node can verify authenticity and freshness independently; replay and forgery die at the door. Crash, restart or migration loses nothing — the protocol is stateless by construction, like JWT, the actor model and Interledger.

Principle 5

Structural termination

Four rules make divergence impossible rather than merely unlikely: gas decreases exactly once per hop; a node seeing itself in the path vector answers LOOP_DETECTED; alignment distance must contract each clarify round (dn+1 ≤ γ·dn); quorum votes over the same top chunk hash collapse to one. Worst case is G + C + R rounds — 6 in the edge profile — and the bound is a theorem about the state machine, not a hope about the network.

Principle 6

Honest cryptography posture

Development runs on HMAC-SHA256 over a canonical serialisation, labelled DEV-SIGNER everywhere it appears; production SHALL use Ed25519 (RFC 8032) signatures and TLS (REQ-S-01). Deploying the dev signer beyond a laboratory LAN is defined as a specification violation, not a configuration. The same honesty applies to every modelled-but-unmeasured number in the cost ledger (§8).

4 · Protocol Mechanics

4.1 Packets and sealing

A packet is a JSON object carrying profile version, action (PROPOSE · BID · ALIGN · RESOLVE · GET_CHUNK · RFO · ACK), origin and query identifiers, sender, remaining gas, a path-vector trail, sealing time, TTL (exp_s, default 60 s), the jti nonce, and an action-specific payload. Sealing adds a signature over the canonical serialisation of the packet — keys sorted, every non-ASCII code unit escaped, and numbers emitted under a strict rule (the pyDouble rule: scientific notation only outside [1e-4, 1e16), two-digit signed exponents, -0.0 preserved, integer-valued floats carry .0). The rule is golden-tested across Python, Kotlin and JavaScript: a signature computed in one language verifies in all others. Replay is handled by caching jti values for the TTL window and silently dropping duplicates; expiry answers an RFO with reason expired — packets die of old age, not only of gas.

4.2 The lifecycle

  • PROPOSE — the origin selects up to k candidates from its discovery directory or peer table (capability-aware: N2/N3 first, N1 as relays and pre-bidders), seals one packet each, sets gas = G.
  • BID — pre-bid gate (firewall layer 1). A responder decides whether to bid without generating: it scores retrieval-side competence (§6.1) and abstains — RFO, NO_QUORUM — when the score falls below bid_min (default 0.15) or it holds no chunk at all. The bid carries bid = 0.5·retrieval + 0.3·reputation + 0.2·top-chunk, node class, generation capability and chunk provenance hashes, not text.
  • ALIGN — lock-in. With C = 1 (edge profile) the origin aligns once with the winner; drift above align_tolerance (0.05) aborts MISMATCH. With C > 1 (full profile) the tolerance contracts by γ = 0.95 per round. Each mapping entry must carry evidence — both concept IRIs plus definitional snippets — and is validated before acceptance (§5.2).
  • RESOLVE — the single generation. The winner charges its budget, generates only inside the envelope (grounding instruction, the query, and cited chunks as quoted data inside <evidence> tags with a directive-stripper on chunk text, REQ-S-03), and returns the answer with cited chunks (hash + text) as provenance. A winner that cannot generate returns an RFO; the origin reports NO_QUORUM rather than accept an ungrounded answer.
  • VERIFY — local entailment and reputation (layer 2/3). The origin computes groundedness g over the cited chunks only. Success raises the sender's reputation by 0.05; failure cuts it by 0.20 and terminates REJECTED. GET_CHUNK/ACK let the origin pull any cited chunk by hash — the only path by which chunk text travels on demand.

4.3 Convergence

Negotiations end in exactly one of six modes — RESOLVED, REJECTED, MISMATCH, GAS_EXHAUSTED, LOOP_DETECTED, NO_QUORUM — within G + C + R rounds (6 in the edge profile: G = 3, C = 1, R = 2). Failure is never silent: RFO packets backtrack along the recorded trail, so the origin always learns the outcome within ≤ 2G hops.

5 · The Hallucination Firewall

Three independent layers, each cheap enough for edge hardware, each anchored in current research. The firewall is complete only when all three run: layer 1 stops incompetent responders before they cost anything; layer 2 stops ungrounded answers; layer 3 makes recidivism expensive.

5.1 Layer 1 — pre-bid competence (responder, zero generation)

The full profile samples the responder k times, clusters samples by bidirectional entailment and computes semantic entropy over meaning clusters [R1] — a high-entropy node exits the auction instead of poisoning it. On edge hardware the cheap variant applies: a linear probe on hidden states predicts semantic entropy in a single forward pass [R2], and the dev-embedder score is query-side coverage, |q ∩ c| / |q| — symmetric Jaccard is unusable here because it collapses when chunk ≫ query. Honest substitution, stated in the spec: cross-chunk NLI agreement approximates, but does not equal, sampled semantic entropy; it can miss a model that confabulates consistently with its retrieved chunks.

5.2 Layer 2 — origin-side groundedness (per answer)

The origin verifies the answer against the cited chunks only. The dev verifier is lexical (fraction of answer tokens present in the evidence, threshold 0.35); the committed production path is a small NLI cross-encoder (DeBERTa-v3-xsmall class, ~70 M, int8) with threshold 0.8 [R5]. Output of any provider — remote or local — is untrusted data. This is the containment that actually bounds hallucination risk when calibration cannot be trusted [R6][R15][R16].

5.3 Layer 3 — reputation and statistical audit

An EWMA per sender enters the bid formula, so unreliable responders are progressively not selected. Above warm reputation, audit intensity scales down (unknown nodes verified 100 %, warm 25 %, trusted every 8th query with mandatory spot audits), and realised groundedness is audited against each node's promised conformal coverage [R3] with a sequential probability ratio test — systematic over-bidding shows up statistically before it shows up as bad answers, and a single audit failure resets the node to 100 % verification. The spec states the trade plainly: audit sampling exchanges a per-message guarantee for a statistical one, deliberately, for energy.

6 · Routing and Discovery

6.1 Centroid routing

No node broadcasts. Each node advertises a codebook of its top-K RAG centroids, quantised to 48 B per centroid (384-d float32 → int8 → binary, asymmetric scoring [A8]), so routing tables stay tiny on edge hardware. The origin matches a 384-bit query code against cached codebooks and proposes to the best-matching candidates only — the centroid-similarity pattern now validated for zero-shot task routing [R13][R11] and for low-latency gating on frozen embedding backbones [R12]. Recent work pushes the same idea further: tool-embedding interpolation toward the centroid of queries where a tool succeeds [R14], and live nearest-centroid event routing for agentic systems [R19]. Verified outcomes drift each advertised centroid toward the query regions where the node actually delivered — codebooks are learned from delivery, not declared.

6.2 Lease-based directory with TOFU

A directory provides register / renew / expire / lookup; a record carries node id, key bundle, endpoints, transports, class, topic codebook and a lease TTL (default 900 s). Registration pins the first key bundle seen per node (Trust On First Use); a changed bundle is rejected until an explicit re-pin. Announcements carry their own jti with the same replay protection as protocol packets. Expired leases disappear from lookup: a departed node vanishes rather than lying about availability.

6.3 Transports: hub and mesh

Two bindings share one transport seam (send / join / peers — three functions, no core changes needed for new transports). In hub mode a WhatsApp group is a topic-scoped channel: its description carries the topic centroid (48 B), one PROPOSE reaches every member in one message, routing collapses to channel selection, and loops are topologically impossible. Messages are two human-readable lines plus an ASCII-safe base64url blob (deterministic CBOR + COSE [A1][A2], ≤ 1.8 KB); chat-speed timers (bid window 10 min, exponential backoff, bounded retries) respect store-and-forward semantics and platform rate tiers [A6]. Transport encryption is not authenticity: Ed25519 signatures remain the source of truth, sensitive topics should enable payload encryption and assume topic names leak. In mesh mode (LAN / Bluetooth) nodes connect directly and run the full profile rules; hub and mesh interoperate, and a query that finds no quorum in its channel may be re-proposed on mesh before threshold decay.

7 · The Decision Ladder — Edge Economics

Most protocol decisions do not need a language model at all. OSP classifies every decision onto a four-rung ladder and enforces one rule: a decision MUST use the lowest rung that suffices.

RungMechanismCost (mid-range phone)OSP decisions
D0vector math: Hamming (popcount) / cosine over binary-quantised embeddings~µs, no model loadedrouting, centroid match, quorum aggregation, similarity scoring
D1linear probes (SEP head [R2]) over embedder hidden states, calibration tables [R3][R15]~0.1 msbid gating (hallucination-risk pre-screen), conformal lookup
D2small cross-encoder NLI (~70 M, int8) [R5]~10–30 ms / pairchunk-agreement gate, payload entailment (VERIFY), audit
D3the SLM itself (0.5–1.5 B, 4-bit) [A4][A5]5–12 s / 200 tokensonly: ontology mapping on cache miss, and the winner's answer

1-bit packing makes the D0 store feasible: 384-d float32 = 1536 B → int8 = 384 B → binary = 48 B (+ fp32 scale); 1 M chunks ≈ 48 MB of vectors plus text, with the query side kept float for asymmetric scoring [A8]. Every D3 call is charged to a daily budget (default 50 generations/day); an exhausted responder still routes and pre-bids but abstains from RESOLVE, and a battery-saver node degrades to D0/D1-only participation — routing and pre-bidding stay alive while generation stops. Ontology mapping is LRU-cached per (peer, concept-pair) with a 7-day TTL and validated evidence [R9][R10]; D3 runs only on a cache miss.

Two operational lessons matter as much as the green rows. First, the negative result: the REJECTED outcome is the protocol working as designed — an ungrounded answer was produced by the generator and stopped at the origin, which is exactly the failure mode calibration-only systems cannot catch [R6][R16]. Second, the honest-abstention path: a query whose indexed evidence cannot support it (« third article », where the corpus has no article numbering) returns INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE/NO_QUORUM behaviour rather than a confident hallucination of an article that does not exist.

10 · Security Analysis

  • Cryptography posture. HMAC-SHA256 and cleartext HTTP are development postures, labelled as such everywhere they appear; production SHALL use Ed25519 (RFC 8032) via COSE/JWS [A2][A9] over TLS. The label is asserted in code and tests, and the deviation is the single explicitly-justified exception to the zero-dependency rule.
  • Packet integrity and freshness. Signatures over canonical bytes; jti replay cache per TTL; expiry answered with an RFO; gas monotonicity and path-vector loops make flooding and bounce attacks structurally bounded.
  • Prompt injection. Evidence is quoted data inside <evidence> tags with an explicit "data, not instructions" clause and a directive-stripper on chunk text (REQ-S-03). The spec calls this hygiene, not defence: the actual containment is origin-side verification — output of any provider is only ever verified, never executed. An adversarial confabulating provider stub proves the property: it wins the auction and is still REJECTED, with reputation decay (REQ-F-02).
  • Privacy. T2 traffic never reaches remote providers by default; chunks travel only when cited or explicitly fetched; the responder's corpus never leaves the node; link secrets authenticate HTTP peering but never sign packets. The Android bridge collects nothing and shares nothing — its data-safety declaration is empty by construction.
  • Known attack surface, stated. Byzantine RFO suppression cannot be fully prevented on hostile paths (fallback: origin's bounded local timeout); key continuity is TOFU, so a directory compromise between pin and re-pin is a substitution window; conformal guarantees assume exchangeability [R3] — distribution shift weakens the bound silently; and the independence assumption behind the quorum error bounds degrades when nodes share corrupted sources beyond what the provenance-diversity rule detects.

11 · Related Work

11.1 Confidence calibration and hallucination detection

Semantic entropy over meaning clusters detects confabulations that token-level agreement misses [R1]; semantic entropy probes make the signal cheap enough for edge hardware [R2]; SelfCheckGPT provides a zero-resource black-box alternative [R5]. Verbalised confidence helps at RLHF scale [R4] but is unreliable on 0.5 B models — the edge profile drops it deliberately. The survey literature consolidates the taxonomy: confidence estimation and calibration methods for LLMs [R15], and uncertainty quantification with risk mitigation and selective prediction [R6]. Most directly relevant to OSP's design stance, Kalai et al. [R16] argue hallucination is incentivised by training and evaluation that reward guessing over abstention — the protocol answer is to make abstention a first-class, rewarded outcome (honest RFO) and guessing an expensive, audited one. Conformal language modelling [R3] supplies the distribution-free coverage guarantees each bid carries.

11.2 Ontology matching (the ALIGN step)

The OAEI campaigns document the hybrid pattern OSP follows: embedding prefiltering to propose candidate correspondences, then an LLM pass to refine and justify each one [R7][R8]. LLMs are known to hallucinate plausible-but-wrong correspondences in domain-specific matching — the OAEI-LLM benchmark quantifies it [R9] — so every OSP mapping entry must carry evidence (both concept IRIs plus definitional snippets) and is validated against the local knowledge graph before acceptance [R10]. Current work confirms both halves of the pattern: LLMs as oracles with validation-based selection reach top ranks in OAEI 2025 [R17], and restricting LLM calls to borderline cases identified by semantic similarity keeps accuracy while containing cost and error [R18] — exactly OSP's cache-first, D3-on-miss policy.

11.3 Centroid and semantic routing

Embedding-based routing without LLM calls [R11], low-latency gating on frozen embedding backbones [R12], and supervised centroid-similarity routing for task selection [R13] establish that a codebook of centroids is a sufficient routing structure at millisecond cost. Outcome-aware interpolation of tool embeddings toward the centroid of successful queries [R14] parallels OSP's delivery-driven centroid drift, and nearest-centroid routing of events for agentic systems [R19] matches OSP's hub-mode channel selection. OSP's contribution is to move this routing structure into a peer-to-peer, adversarial setting where the routed entities are untrusted knowledge holders rather than local tools.

12 · Limitations

  • Independence is an assumption. The quorum error bounds degrade if nodes share corrupted sources beyond what provenance diversity detects (two wrong manuals that agree with each other).
  • The semantic-entropy gate is a proxy in the edge profile: retrieval-side agreement can miss a model that confabulates consistently with its retrieved chunks; the full gate needs sampled generations and bigger models.
  • Audit sampling trades per-message guarantees for statistical bounds — an economy, stated as such, with a hard reset on any audit failure.
  • Centroid staleness: advertised codebooks lag RAG updates; routing recall degrades gracefully, not instantly.
  • Calibration drift: bid weights and thresholds need periodic re-validation on real traffic or the firewall slowly goes soft [R6]; conformal bounds assume exchangeability [R3].
  • All cost numbers are modelled until L6 runs on target hardware; the spec refuses to present estimates as measurements.
  • Dev transports: cleartext HTTP and the HMAC dev signer confine current deployments to laboratory LANs by specification, not by convention.

13 · Conclusion and Future Work

OSP demonstrates that the verification burden of small-model knowledge exchange can be carried by protocol structure instead of model introspection: a stateless signed envelope, a generation-free bid gate, an origin-side groundedness check and a reputation economy together give sub-2 B models a way to share knowledge that fails loud instead of wrong. The design is fully specified, implemented three times in three languages, pinned by 87 conformance tests and validated live across a three-language chain — including the rejection path that most RAG systems quietly lack.

Next steps, in specification order: the Ed25519 + TLS production signer (REQ-S-01, the one dependency worth making); a real NLI verifier behind the existing D2 interface (ONNX int8 cross-encoder); L5 property/fuzz testing of the termination invariant over random seeded topologies; transport adapters (WhatsApp hub, BLE mesh) behind the existing three-function seam, unlocking the L6 hardware benchmark that converts the modelled cost ledger into measurements.
